Introduction – Simulated Tempering

Tempering: Lift the barriers

\[ \text{Introduce an} \textbf{ inverse-temperature } \color{purple}{\beta} : \qquad \pi_{\color{purple}{\beta}}(x)\propto\pi(x)^{\color{purple}{\beta}}, \qquad \color{purple}{\beta}\in(0,1]. \]

Cold target (\(\beta=1\)) and warmer distributions (\(\beta\ll1\)).

Multiple Targets

  • Key idea: a chain that moves vertically can escape modes at low \(\beta\) …
  • … and bring that information back to the target level \(\beta_0=1\) [KT90; MP92; GT95].

Simulated Tempering : A Metropolis-within-Gibbs scheme

  • Within-temperature move: any kernel (RWM, MALA, HMC, etc) that leaves \(\pi_{\beta_k}\) invariant.
  • Between-temperature move: a kernel \(T\) that updates \(\beta\).

How do we choose \(\color{#FF8C00}{\ell}\)?

How can we improve?

Algorithm 1 Guided walk (one step)
  1. draw \(U\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)\)
  2. if \(U<\min\{1,R_{k,\,k+\color{#1565c0}{\nu}}\}\) then
  3. \(\color{#1565c0}{k\leftarrow k+\nu}\)
  4. else
  5. \(\color{#c62828}{\nu\leftarrow-\nu}\)
  6. end if

\[ R_{k,j}=\frac{\pi_{\color{blue}{\beta_{j}}}(x)}{\pi_{\color{red}{\beta_k}}(x)}. \]

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{eff}(\ell)=\color{orange}{\ell^2}\left(\color{blue}{a(\ell)}+\frac{\color{green}{d(\ell)}^2}{\color{red}{\rho(\ell)}}\right)\)

  • \(\color{green}{d(\ell)}=a(\ell)\) is maximal: momentum direction only.
  • \(\color{red}{\rho(\ell)}=1-a(\ell)\): can the flip rate be reduced?

Can we minimize flips further?

Algorithm 2 Lazy guided walk (one step)
  1. draw a single \(U\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)\)
  2. if \(U<\min\{1,R_{k,\,k+\color{#1565c0}{\nu}}\}\) then
  3. \(\color{#1565c0}{k\leftarrow k+\nu}\)
  4. else if \(U<\min\{1,R_{k,\,k-\color{#1565c0}{\nu}}\}\) then
  5. \(\color{#c62828}{\nu\leftarrow-\nu}\)
  6. else
  7. \(\color{#2e7d32}{\text{stay and keep }\nu}\) ▷ lazy step
  8. end if

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{eff}(\ell)=\color{orange}{\ell^2}\left(\color{blue}{a(\ell)}+\frac{\color{green}{d(\ell)}^2}{\color{red}{\rho(\ell)}}\right)\)

  • Drift: backward moves possible.
  • Flips: stronger persistence.
  • Acceptance: higher acceptance if the with-momentum transition stays intact.


Can we trade a little drift for substantially more persistence?

Yielding Guided Walk [QRT26+]

Allow a backward, opposite-momentum proposal with probability

\[ q^-= \frac{1}{\pi_k} \left[ \min\{\pi_k,\color{#c62828}{\pi_{k-1}}\}-\max\{\color{#1565c0}{\pi_{k+1}},\color{#8a8a99}{\pi_{k-2}}\} \right]_+, \qquad \pi_j := \pi(\beta_j \mid x) \]

\(\color{#1565c0}{\pi_{k+1}}\ge \color{#c62828}{\pi_{k-1}}\)
Forward is at least as good — never yield.
\(q^- = 0\)
\(\color{#8a8a99}{\pi_{k-2}}\ge \pi_k\)
Backward impossible — try forward anyway.
\(q^- = 0\)
\(\color{#8a8a99}{\pi_{k-2}}\le \color{#1565c0}{\pi_{k+1}}\le \color{#c62828}{\pi_{k-1}}\)
Yield by the gap to \(\color{#1565c0}{\pi_{k+1}}\).
\(q^- = \dfrac{\min\{\pi_k,\color{#c62828}{\pi_{k-1}}\}-\color{#1565c0}{\pi_{k+1}}}{\pi_k}\)
\(\color{#1565c0}{\pi_{k+1}}\le \color{#8a8a99}{\pi_{k-2}}\le \color{#c62828}{\pi_{k-1}}\)
Yield by the gap to \(\color{#8a8a99}{\pi_{k-2}}\).
\(q^- = \dfrac{\min\{\pi_k,\color{#c62828}{\pi_{k-1}}\}-\color{#8a8a99}{\pi_{k-2}}}{\pi_k}\)

A Gaussian prior and likelihood keep the tempered target Gaussian at every level:

\[ \pi_0=\mathrm N(0,\sigma_0^2 I),\qquad \ell(x)\propto e^{-\|x-m\|^2/2} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \pi^{(\beta)}=\mathrm N\!\big(\mu_\beta\,\mathbf 1,\; \sigma_\beta^2 I\big), \]

\[ \sigma_\beta^2=\big(\beta+\sigma_0^{-2}\big)^{-1}, \qquad \mu_\beta=\beta\,m\,\sigma_\beta^2 . \]

Toy multivariate Gaussian with AR(1)

To model an imperfect within-temperature move, replace the exact refresh by a single AR(1) step with correlation \(\rho\):

\[ X^{(t+1)} = \mu(\beta^{(t)}) + \rho\big( X^{(t)} - \mu(\beta^{(t)}) \big) + \sigma(\beta^{(t)})\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,\mathbf{Z}^{(t+1)}, \qquad \mathbf{Z}^{(t+1)} \sim \mathrm N(0,I). \]

Takeaways & Further Work

Takeaways

  • Reversible temperature kernels hit an efficiency ceiling — closely approached by informed proposals (Barker, \(\sqrt{\cdot}\)-balanced).
  • Naive non-reversibility (plain Guided Walk) is dominated by the best reversible kernels.
  • Cutting the flip rate — Lazy Guided Walk — beats every reversible method.
  • Allowing an opposite-momentum move — Yielding Guided Walk — is optimal among nearest-neighbour skew-MH temperature kernels.

Simulated Tempering mixing stays inherently bounded.

Future Work — Diagonal Moves

Illustration motivating diagonal temperature-state moves, Grenioux & Noble, 2026.

  • Diagonal moves: Update temperature and state jointly [TR19; GN26] could break the efficiency ceiling. Potential ideas span Reversible-Jump MCMC, normalizing flows …

Future Work — Reference Distributions and Paths

Weight degeneracy in Tempering, Tawn et. al, 2019.

  • Reference distribution: global tempering degrades to mode collapse [WSH09]. Seek a weight-preserving scheme [TRR19], a tractable approximation to the target [TMQR25], a variational reference [SSBC22]?
